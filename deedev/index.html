Overview

What the company does, and the one-line verdict on each dimension.

Bottom line: a fundamentally improving company at a technically weak moment. Record orders and new capacity support the story, but the share price is below its key trend line and cash generation is poor.

What DEE does

DEE builds the high-pressure pipe systems that run inside power stations, refineries and petrochemical plants. It designs, buys, bends, welds, tests and ships custom pipe sections (spools) ready to install.

Founded 1988. Its protection from rivals (moat) is qualification: new customers audit a supplier for up to 24 months, and once approved, DEE tends to stay on their vendor list for a decade or more.

Business segments

Process piping (core)

Custom pipe spools, bends and pressure vessels for power, oil & gas and process industries.

FY26 core revenue ₹1,086.5 Cr (+46%) · 730+ engineers · ~40% bid win rate

Heavy fabrication

Wind turbine towers and industrial chimney stacks via DEE Fabricom.

First tower order ~₹90 Cr · ₹64 Cr for fifteen 3.3 MW towers (June 2026)

Seamless pipes (new)

7,000 MTPA Anjar plant making thick-walled alloy pipes (P-91/P-92) for 660/800 MW supercritical boilers.

Started 19 Mar 2026 · ~₹89.74 Cr invested · NTPC approved · first order ~₹58 Cr

Biomass power (non-core)

8 MW Abohar + 6 MW Muktsar plants, shifting to fuel pellets.

Tariff raised to ₹5.22/kWh from ₹3.50 · 72,000 MTPA pellet plant live Q1 FY27 · ~₹80 Cr FY27 target

Plants

  • Anjar (30,000 MTPA, near Kandla/Mundra ports) handles oil & gas and export work, freeing Palwal for higher-margin power jobs.
  • Thailand (14,500 MTPA) is the export showcase. No expansion planned there; all new capacity goes to India.

Theme

Engineering-led piping for India's energy spending cycle.

Core theme: a revival in coal-fired (thermal) power plant building. Announced boiler units exceed the industry's current fabrication capacity, which supports steady orders for DEE.

Design-led, not commodity work

DEE sells guaranteed piping systems and gets each layout approved before cutting any pipe. Getting through the customer's qualification door is the moat.

Thermal power revival

Adani, JSW and L&T are announcing new thermal projects. BHEL's ~53 units of 800/660 MW plus L&T's ~23 units exceed existing industry fabrication capacity.

Global gas-turbine supply chain

  • A Reservation Agreement with an international EPC major (Nooter Eriksen) books 60% of Thailand's heat-recovery boiler (HRSG) capacity at a minimum of US$15.27 Mn/year, June 2027 to December 2029.
  • A US$40 Mn+ letter of intent (LOI) from a global equipment maker (OEM) marks entry into global supply chains.

Making its own pipes (backward integration)

The seamless pipe plant replaces pipes earlier imported entirely from China/Europe. It targets ~20% operating margin (EBITDA) at full use, mainly for coal boiler work rather than HRSG.

Energy-transition options

  • Hydrogen purification via a 70% stake in Molsieve Designs
  • Carbon-capture piping supplies
  • Nuclear sector entry targeted via an agreement in Q2 FY27

Financial track record, order book and capacity ramp.

Visibility: ₹2,428 Cr order book gives nearly two years of work. FY26 operating profit (EBITDA) grew 54.5% to ₹191.2 Cr at a 16.7% margin.

Growth catalysts

Order book ₹2,428 Cr

FY26 closed at ₹1,940 Cr (+58% YoY) plus ₹211 Cr where DEE is lowest bidder (L1). FY27 new-order guidance is over ₹2,000 Cr; ~₹780 Cr already came in Q1.

BPCL order ₹386.82 Cr

One of the largest single orders in recent history, delivering by February 2028.

Anjar ramp-up

~50% of capacity used in Q1 FY27; target 60–65% in FY27 and near 100% by FY28. Peak revenue potential ~₹1,500 Cr from Anjar alone.

Seamless pipe plant

FY27 use guided at 60–70%, split 50-50 between own use and outside sale. ~₹450 Cr revenue potential at ~20% EBITDA margin and 30–35% return on investment (IRR).

GE heat-recovery boilers (HRSG)

Agreement for 15–16 units in FY27. Even without new GE orders, DEE is booked for over 1.5 years.

Siemens gas-turbine piping

10 units FY27, 15 FY28, 25–30 FY29 at ~€1–1.5 Mn each, pending Anjar plant approval.

Thailand

Practically 100% booked for three years. FY27 revenue target ₹170–200 Cr vs ₹130 Cr last year.

Vision 2030 (now pulled to FY29)

₹2,500 Cr revenue, 19–20% EBITDA margin, 9–10% net margin, asset turns (sales per ₹1 of assets) ~3.5x.

Watch: ~₹25 Cr of Q1 dispatches slipped to Q2 as Middle East export customers deferred. The ~₹380 Cr GE order had no drawing inputs as of June 2026, so drawing release is the key FY27 swing factor.

Valuation

Priced for execution: the market pays for the FY27–30 turnaround, not today's returns.

Key point: the stock costs 45.4x its profit (P/E), 61% above the industry's 28.2x, while the company earns only 9.2% on capital employed (ROCE) and 8.7% on equity (ROE).

PEG looks fair only if growth delivers

A PEG (P/E ÷ growth rate) of 0.79 assumes guidance is met: ₹1,500 Cr revenue and >19% EBITDA margin. A miss on GE drawings or the Anjar ramp would leave little cushion.

Negative free cash flow

The -4.0% free cash flow yield reflects the building phase. The bull case rests on FY27 being the first year of light spending and cash generation from higher plant use (FY27 capex guided at only ₹20–30 Cr).

Risk

What could go wrong: cash, audits, working capital, concentration, debt and disputes.

Biggest risk: profit is not turning into cash. FY26 cash from operations (CFO) was ₹19.55 Cr, only 0.25x the ₹77 Cr net profit.

Free cash flow (₹ Cr)

Cash flow lags profit

Free cash flow (FCF) was -₹159 Cr in FY26 and -₹281 Cr in FY25, on capex and inventory build. Money tied up in operations (working capital) is ₹861 Cr; borrowings ₹771 Cr.

Auditor reservations two years running

S.R. Batliboi qualified the FY25 and FY26 consolidated opinions over an unquantified write-down (impairment) at Malwa Power; ~₹47.6 Cr of assets untested. Management has committed to a clean opinion by Q3 FY27.

Slow cash cycle

Q1 FY27: 263 days from paying for materials to collecting from customers (cash conversion cycle), vs a 180–200 day target. Days to collect from customers rose from 109 to 123. Eight customers account for 71% of money owed.

Client & sector concentration

Revenue is concentrated in oil & gas and power. Export customers deferred deliveries in Q1 on Middle East tensions.

Debt & credit rating

CARE A- / Stable / A2+ reaffirmed on enhanced facilities (long-term limits raised to ₹205 Cr and ₹673 Cr). Net debt ₹718 Cr at Q1 FY27, guided to ₹400–425 Cr by FY27-end after the preferential issue.

Legal & tariff disputes

PSPCL appeal and APTEL hearings on the Malwa tariff unresolved. A FEMA notice (May 2023) and a CESTAT customs demand (~₹11.2 Cr plus penalty) are pending.

Why it fails Stage 2

Stage 2 needs price above a rising 30-week average with positive relative strength. Here price is ~10% below the 30-week average, below every daily average, 30.8% off its high, and lagging the Nifty 500 over 26 weeks.

Why it is not Stage 4

The 30-week average is still above the 40-week, and the golden cross from 33 weeks ago is still active. The long-term uptrend structure is intact.

What to watch: RSI at 29 is oversold (often precedes a bounce). A return to Stage 2 needs price to reclaim the 30-week average (~₹586) with relative strength turning positive.
