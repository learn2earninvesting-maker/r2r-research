The verdict

What the report concludes, in one screen.

Bottom line: Jash owns a genuinely scarce global niche. FY26 was a shock from outside (US tariffs and the Middle East war), and FY27 guidance looks achievable. But at ~40x earnings, with profits running ahead of cash for a decade, the market has already paid up front for good execution.

What works

  • Owns respected global brands: Rodney Hunt (US) and Mahr (Austria).
  • About 70% of the order book is international.
  • FY27 target of ₹875 Cr, backed by a ₹932 Cr order book.
  • Low debt: borrowings only 0.20x of shareholders' money (D/E).
  • Share price in a confirmed uptrend (Stage 2).

What to watch

  • Price is ~40x earnings vs a 3-year median of 11.3x (P/E).
  • Little actual cash left after investment: 0.6% (FCF yield).
  • Over 10 years, cash from operations covered only half of operating profit.
  • Missed its own margin and FY26 sales targets.
  • Repeated auditor remarks on bank statements not matching the books.

How to read this

The company is likely heading the right way. The risk is not the direction but how fragile the high price is if profit margins miss the 13–14% target again.

The hidden muscle of water infrastructure

What Jash makes, and why few can copy it.

Jash makes the huge gates, screens and valves that control water flowing through dams, treatment plants and drains. Founded in 1948 in Dewas; listed on NSE in 2017.

What it sells

ProductShare of salesNotes
Water control gates~60%Penstocks and fabricated gates. Management says ~70% market share and the only listed player.
Screening equipment~15–16%Mahr (Austria, est. 1927) invented bar-screen technology; such screens are called "Mahr-type" worldwide.
Valves~8–17%Knife gate valves, zero-velocity valves, air vessels.
Other~7–16%Archimedes screw pumps, WesTech process equipment, Invent disc filters.

Chart uses the midpoint of each range shown in the report.

Why the niche is hard to attack (moat)

  • Size of work: can cast single pieces up to 20 tonnes, machine 10-metre parts and build 50-tonne jobs. Few competitors worldwide can match this.
  • Owned global brands: Rodney Hunt (US, bought 2016) was the #1 US gates name for 50+ years till 2015. Mahr carries the screening technology legacy.
  • One roof: design, casting, machining, fabrication, assembly and testing all in-house.

The shift abroad

Exports were 64% of FY25 sales. About 70% of the order book is international (₹650 Cr of ₹923 Cr at that point).

Subsidiaries: US (Rodney Hunt, Orange MA), UK (Waterfront Glasgow + Penstocks Midlands), Austria (Mahr), Hong Kong, Saudi Arabia (Dammam plant planned).

How US tariffs hit Jash: Rodney Hunt takes the US order. When it supplies from inside the US, no tariff. When it buys parts from Jash India, Rodney Hunt pays the tariff. Tariffs went 0% → 25% (Jan) → 50% (Jun), then were cut to 10% in Feb 2026 after a US Supreme Court intervention.

Orders and capacity

  • Order book: ₹932 Cr (₹639 Cr outside India). About 85% of the ₹912 Cr won can be delivered within FY27. New orders usually run 1.2–1.5x sales (book-to-bill).
  • Capacity: ~₹70 Cr of India spending (capex) supports ₹1,200 Cr of sales from current plants. The SEZ Pithampur plant (Apr 2026) and Unit 1 foundry expansion (Q1 FY27) lift cast gate/valve capacity by over 30%.

New options (optionality)

  • Data-centre pressure vessels: first order received; 600+ vessels quoted at ₹30–60 lakh each.
  • Singapore sea-level projects: quoted over USD 90 Cr, 20–40% share of Jash-relevant equipment, execution from 2028 over 20–25 years.
  • Mahr Austria: reviving, with a ₹31 Cr order book.
Can you trust the guidance? Mixed. FY24 and FY25 sales targets were met or beaten. But FY25 margin guidance (EBITDA 21–23%, PAT 13–13.5%) was missed, FY26 sales guidance was cut twice and missed, and the Houston plant keeps slipping (construction not started as of Q1 FY27). Sales targets: trust them. Margin and timeline targets: discount them.

The re-rating is extreme on P/E: 39.9x vs a 3-year median of 11.3x. The market has paid up front for the US expansion and order visibility. There's no safety cushion for another FY26-style miss.

EV/EBITDA looks less stretched

30.2x vs a 28.5x median, because FY26 operating profit was depressed. On normal FY27–28 earnings, this multiple shrinks meaningfully.

Cash is the real problem

Over 10 years, cash from operations (₹281 Cr) covered only half of operating profit (₹560 Cr). In FY26, cash from operations was 0.74x net profit.

A 0.6% free-cash yield at 40x earnings means the stock depends on money stuck in day-to-day operations (working capital) actually coming back.

Clean of fraud, not of lapses

Who owns it, who runs it, and the governance record.

Ownership

Promoters 43.24% with zero shares pledged. Foreign institutions 1.54%, domestic institutions 0.32%. This is a retail/HNI-owned stock with almost no institutional backing.

Source: latest market data. "Others" = remainder to 100%.

Promoter stake has fallen from 49.44% (Mar 2024) to 43.24%. Watch whether it keeps falling.

Family control

  • 83-year-old Suresh Patel re-appointed Executive Director at up to ₹7 lakh/month via postal ballot (99.94% in favour).
  • Pratik Patel's MD pay raised to ₹25 lakh/month; his five-year re-appointment goes to a shareholder vote effective 1 Mar 2027.

Repeated auditor remarks

  • FY26 annual report: auditor flags that stock and receivable statements filed with banks don't match the books, due to inventory adjustments.
  • FY24: quantified mismatches, the largest an ₹11.17 Cr receivables gap (Jun 2023).

Cash and related-party flags

  • A loan to a subsidiary was extended at maturity, with ₹1.85 Cr interest overdue for six years.
  • ₹6.86 Cr of overseas dues overdue beyond 15 months; RBI extension pending.
  • ₹30 Cr+ of acquisition goodwill never written down, even though Mahr's board approved its liquidation in 2020.
  • ₹18.09 Cr fixed deposits are under lien; all term loans carry personal guarantees from the Patels.
No fraud allegation exists in the audited record. The pattern is chronic sloppiness and timing management, not theft. But for a 40x P/E stock, governance is a negative, not a support.

Risks & catalysts

What could push the stock up, and what could break the story.

Catalysts

Tariff refund

USD 1.5–2.5M claimed, plus a further $1M flagged in Jul 2026. Worth ₹12–20 Cr straight to profit, adding 1–1.5% to FY27 net margin.

Houston plant (₹115 Cr)

Making in the US would remove the tariff drag entirely. Targeted before Dec 2027.

US municipal spending

Rodney Hunt targets $60M+ by 2030-31 in a North American market of over $150M for its products.

Operating leverage

As sales grow faster than fixed costs, margins can expand. Management targets EBITDA 21–24% and PAT 13–14% at scale; it has hit 12.5% PAT before.

New streams

Singapore, Saudi and data-centre vessels: three new revenue lines with multi-year visibility.

Risks

Working capital is structural. 12–24 months from tender to delivery; customers historically take 120–185 days to pay. FY26 working capital was ₹392 Cr on ₹736 Cr of sales. Management: "a lot of the cash is stuck in working capital."

Guidance slippage

FY26 sales and margin targets both missed; Houston has slipped repeatedly.

Tariffs and geopolitics

The 50% tariff forced Jash to cut US production mid-year. Today's 10% rests on a Supreme Court intervention that could be revisited.

Stuck shipments

Singapore (credit hold despite a letter of credit) and Qatar shipments, ~₹15 Cr, unresolved as of Q1 FY27.

Raw materials

Fixed-price orders expose margins to steel prices; management argues a weaker rupee offsets it.

Credit rating

Agency rating (CRISIL/ICRA/CARE): NOT DISCLOSED

Bank facilities are disclosed (SBI, ICICI, Axis; ₹57.6 Cr current borrowings at Mar 2026), but no agency rationale is on record.
