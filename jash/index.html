What it does [F]

Designs, casts, builds and supplies equipment that controls water flow: sluice and penstock gates, screens, valves and pumping/process equipment. Customers are municipal water, wastewater and industrial plants in India, and abroad through subsidiaries in the US, UK and Austria. (Screener About)

Where it is now [F]

FY26 revenue was ₹736 cr, flat on FY25's ₹735 cr. Profit after tax (PAT) fell 13% to ₹76 cr (Screener). In Jan/Feb 2026 the company had projected a revised ₹775-800 cr.

What drives the stock from here [O]

Whether it hits its FY27 targets of ₹875 cr revenue and ₹100-105 cr PAT. These rest on a ₹944 cr order book (work already won but not yet delivered).

What is most likely to go wrong [O]

A second year of missing its own forecast. Likely causes: export shipments slipping, losses at Rodney Hunt (US) and at the newly bought process-equipment unit. At ~40 times last-12-months earnings, there is no cushion for that.

1. Sectoral theme

What industry Jash sits in, how steady its demand is, and where its sales come from.

The theme

Jash is part of the water-infrastructure story: town water supply and sewage treatment, flood control and desalination (turning seawater into drinking water) in India, plus replacement of US town water systems through its Rodney Hunt subsidiary.

[O] The demand is real and long-lived, but it comes in lumps. It depends on government project budgets and on the timing of the big contractors who build these plants (EPC: engineering, procurement and construction).

No independent source on industry size was available, so the size of this tailwind cannot be measured here NOT AVAILABLE (needs an industry report).

[F] In May 2025 the company put India's water-infrastructure opportunity at ₹1,500 cr+ (May 2025 investor presentation). That is the company's own estimate.

Steady trend or boom-and-bust? (structural vs cyclical)

[E] Revenue rose in 10 of the last 11 years (Screener; FY16 and FY26 flat), and earnings do not follow any commodity price.

But the profit kept from each ₹100 of sales (operating profit margin, OPM) swings a lot: from 5% (FY18) to 19% (FY24) (Screener).

FY26 showed the swing comes from the mix of exports and from world events, not from demand. India's share of revenue rose from 37% in FY25 to 44.5% in FY26, while uncertainty over US import taxes (tariffs) and the West Asia conflict dented profits. US tariffs moved to 25% and then to 50%.

Verdict [O]: Sales volume growth is structural (long-term), but margins are cyclical and exposed to sudden events.

Can it hold its prices? (pricing-power test)

The test: did the profit left after paying for materials (gross margin) hold up in the last bad year?

[F] In FY26, with flat revenue and falling profit, gross margin actually improved to 57.0% from 55.7%. So pricing held.

Operating profit (EBITDA) fell because fixed costs stayed while sales dropped (operating deleverage): a 12% revenue fall at Rodney Hunt became a 44% drop in its profit.

Gross margin did swing between quarters: 49.4% in Q4FY25 and 50.8% in Q1FY26, against ~60% in Q4FY26 and Q1FY27 (second-hand summaries of results). FY18 gross margin: NOT AVAILABLE.

[O] What protects the business: a range of large custom sizes, its own foundry, and decades of approvals with the consultants who write project specifications. That makes customers reluctant to switch, but it is not a proven moat (lasting competitive advantage).

b. What drives revenue

For a capital-goods maker, sales = order book (work won) × how fast it is delivered (execution rate).

[F] Order book history:

₹784 cr (1 Apr 2024), ₹873 cr (Nov 2024 concall), ₹838 cr (1 May 2025, IP), ₹827 cr (1 Apr 2026), ₹899 cr (1 May 2026), ₹932 cr (1 Aug 2026), ₹944 cr (1 Oct 2026).

The latest ₹944 cr book splits ₹293 cr within India and ₹639 cr outside India. By entity: Jash ₹559 cr, Rodney Hunt ₹369 cr, Waterfront ₹34 cr, Jash Process Equipment ₹29 cr, Mahr ₹31 cr.

[E] Order book compared with last-12-months revenue (book-to-bill) is 944 ÷ 758 = 1.25x.

The order book grew only ~8% in roughly two years (₹873 cr in Nov 2024 to ₹944 cr in Oct 2026), while revenue was flat. That is a steady state, not acceleration. Management itself cautioned that the order book does not turn one-to-one into same-year sales.

[F] Capacity: The Unit 1 foundry and valve expansion was completed in July 2026, raising casting capacity by 50% and valve capacity by over 30%. A second source gives the gate-and-valve increase as "over 30 percent". How much of the capacity is being used (utilisation): NOT DISCLOSED.

c. Quality of growth

  • Does profit turn into cash? [E] Total cash from operations over FY17-26 is ₹301 cr against total PAT of ₹383 cr, a ratio of 0.79. That passes the 0.7 bar, but only just. Total free cash flow (cash left after investment) is ₹96 cr, just 25% of PAT (Screener).
  • Money owed by customers (receivables) [F][E]: the average days customers take to pay (debtor days) rose from 112 (FY25) to 149 (FY26). Cash tied up in day-to-day operations (working-capital days) rose from 71 to 103 (Screener). [E] Receivables went from about ₹226 cr (112/365 × 735) to about ₹300 cr (149/365 × 736), up ~33% while revenue was flat. Part of this is March-heavy billing. Either way it is a red flag until the H1FY27 balance sheet shows the money collected.
  • Return on new money invested [E] (EBIT = PBT + interest; capital employed = equity + reserves + borrowings, Screener): over 3 years (FY23→26) extra profit ₹37 cr ÷ extra capital ₹301 cr = 12%. Over 5 years (FY21→26) 57 ÷ 394 = 14%. Both are well below the reported 25% ROCE for FY24-25. New capital, including the FY24 share issue and acquisitions, is earning about half the historical return.
  • Income from outside the core business and one-offs: other income was ₹20 cr in FY26, 22% of the ₹90 cr profit before tax (PBT) (Screener). In Q1FY27, other income of ₹6.13 cr was almost all of PBT (₹6.54 cr). A tariff refund of ₹5.6 cr helped margins, and about ₹7.5 cr more is expected. The Q1 "turnaround" was largely one-off. Quarterly tax rates are erratic too: -1.24% in Q3FY26, -121% in Q1FY25 (Screener).
  • Sales carried over (spill-over) [F][E]: about ₹35 cr of March 2026 shipments missed the shipping-terms (Incoterms) cut-off and will count in Q1FY27 revenue. [E] If all of it landed in Q1, underlying Q1FY27 revenue was about ₹115 cr (149.9 − 35), roughly 10% below Q1FY26's ₹127.6 cr. The exact amount booked was not confirmed in the sources available.

  1. FY27 guidance missed for a second year. Middle East and US shipments slip and March billing disappoints. Early warning: H1FY27 revenue below ₹310 cr. Then Qatar shipments stuck for over 3 months start repeating.
