Sectoral Theme

The machine-tool maker behind India's factory build-out.

From gearboxes to jet-engine machines

Promoters started in 1989 making gearboxes and began building CNC machines in 2002. The turning point: buying Huron Graffenstaden, a 150-year-old French pioneer of 5-axis machines (machines that cut from five directions at once, needed for complex aerospace parts), in 2007 — when Jyoti's revenue was only ₹50 Cr.

One engine, three price tiers

About 93% of revenue is project-based machine sales, across 200+ models:

Who buys (FY26 revenue mix)

Customers include HAL, ISRO, Tata Boeing, Airbus, Safran, GE and Rolls Royce. "Other" is the remaining 12% not broken out in the report.

Makes most parts itself

In-house foundry, machine shop, spindles, tool changers and rotary tables (vertically integrated). Only ~10% of material spend — controllers and guideways — is imported.

Huron = the technology gateway

The Strasbourg plant sits within 200 km of every major global machine-tool builder, and its brand opens doors with global aerospace/defence firms. A new facility doubled its capacity in Nov 2025.

Tailwinds: Make in India import substitution, defence indigenisation, electronics/semiconductor incentives and the EV shift. India's electronics manufacturing (EMS) sector alone is expected to need 1,00,000+ CNC machines in five years. Jyoti holds an estimated 10–12% domestic share in high-precision metal-cutting CNC.

Margin story is real, growth is slowing. Margin rose from 8.3% (FY23) to 25%+ thanks to more high-end machines and the Huron turnaround. But FY26 growth halved to 15.2%, and management cut its 30–35% growth guidance to 25–30%. Q1 FY27 revenue grew 24%, but margin dipped to 21.4% (23.4% adjusted) on Huron accounting changes and currency losses.

Order book (Mar 2026): ₹4,732 Cr

  • 18–20 months of revenue visibility; ~40% are high-end machines (above ₹2 Cr) that carry better margins
  • FY27 new orders guided at ₹2,500–3,000 Cr; Q1 FY27 intake ~₹600 Cr is on track
  • Factories ran at ~88% use, hitting 100%+ in peak FY26 months; FY26 output 5,550 machines vs 6,000 capacity

Capacity catalysts

  • Rajkot mega-expansion: 6,000 → 16,000 machines/year via ₹400–450 Cr; ~80% built, commissioning on track for end-Sep 2026; FY27 output guided at 8,000–9,000 machines. At full use and ~₹30 lakh average price, this nearly triples the revenue base.
  • Huron doubling: ~240 machines/year since Nov 2025, enabling €70–75 Mn of revenue vs €30–32 Mn before; FY27 Huron revenue guided ₹300–325 Cr.
  • Deferred revenue: ~₹100 Cr (₹67 Cr + ₹35 Cr) of billed Huron machines await export licences; the ~₹40 Cr margin hit reverses once booked.
  • Electronics (EMS) optionality: a ₹700 Cr EMS order book has sat static for 1.5–2 years; target is 20–25% of business vs just 1% of FY26 revenue.
  • In-house controllers (HUMA): prototype due in 12–18 months; replacing ~30% imported content guides a 4–5% gross margin gain. MeitY approved a ₹1,020.65 Cr Rajkot investment proposal on 17 Aug 2026 with up to 25% capex incentive.
  • New products & markets: NX 6240 EY double-column machine (₹3–5 Cr each) for railways/heavy engineering; a ₹200 Cr MBDA order expected; US sales office expected within 2–3 months.

Risks

Cash burn, rising debt and a French investigation.

1. Negative free cash flow

Free cash flow (cash left after running and expanding the business) was -₹415 Cr in FY25 and -₹269 Cr in FY26. Only 16.2% of FY26 profit turned into operating cash (OCF/PAT). Target for FY27: operating cash at ~50% of EBITDA vs ~10% in FY26 — a big jump.

2. Debt coming back

Net debt ₹716 Cr; debt-to-equity 0.43 vs 0.22 in FY24. The FY24 promise of being debt-free in 2–3 years is dead.

3. Cash stuck in the business

Inventory (stock held) ran 200–240 days by Q3 FY26 vs a 150–160 day target. Unbilled revenue (work done but not yet invoiced) grew to ~₹673 Cr in H1 FY26.

4. Huron judicial investigation (Apr 2026): French authorities are investigating suspected export of dual-use machines (usable for civilian and military purposes) in breach of EU law. Huron's director general is temporarily restricted and ~€4.0 Mn of bank accounts are seized. The company says there is no adverse impact (standalone Jyoti is 85%+ of group revenue), will contest strongly, and expects no impairment by end-FY27 — but it is an unresolved overhang on export-licence-dependent Huron revenue.

5. Execution slippage

The Rajkot deadline has slipped twice: Dec 2025 → Jun 2026 → Sep 2026. A minor fire occurred at the Rajkot facility in Sep 2026.

6. Falling price per machine

Average realization (price per machine) fell from a peak of ₹50.27 lakh (Q3 FY25) to ₹34.56 lakh in Q1 FY27 — below the ₹40 lakh floor management had committed to.

7. Guidance misses & cyclicality

EMS contribution, inventory days, interest savings and the 30–35% growth target have all been missed. Heavy dependence on aerospace/defence and auto capital spending adds cyclicality.
