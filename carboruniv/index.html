What CUMI does

A materials-science company that owns the chain from raw mineral to finished product ("Mines to Market").

CUMI turns minerals into surfaces: the grit that grinds, the ceramics that armour and insulate, and advanced powders going into fuel cells and semiconductors. Founded in 1954, it kept buying its way back into raw materials and power (backward integration) before moving up to higher-value products.

Ceramics earns about ₹20 of profit on every ₹100 of sales (20.2% PBIT margin). The other two big businesses earn only ₹4–6. That gap is the whole investment story.

Revenue vs margin by segment

Abrasives

Grinding wheels, sandpaper-type products and super-hard abrasives. Sold through ~18,000 retail outlets in India and in 55+ countries. Its raw grain (fused alumina, silicon carbide) comes from CUMI's own Electrominerals unit. The 2022 German purchases (Rhodius, Awuko) added European factories but have dragged profit down.

Electrominerals

Runs its own 12 MW hydro power plant at Maniyar and bauxite mines in Gujarat, and owns the world's largest single-site silicon carbide plant (90,000 tonnes a year) at VAW in Russia. The product mix is being moved away from commodities (85% of the basket today) toward treated grains, specialties and high-purity silicon carbide.

Ceramics

Wear-resistant linings; metallised cylinders for vacuum interrupters (grid switchgear parts) where CUMI is world #2, supplying ABB, Siemens and Schneider; ballistic armour qualified to NATO STANAG 4; and a new ₹66 Cr Hosur facility making ceramics for semiconductor fab equipment.

Credit standing

CRISIL re-affirmed AA+/Stable (long-term) and A1+ (short-term) ratings. The standalone Indian parent has more cash than debt (net debt-free).

Revenue vs net profit

Q2 FY27 was the turning-point quarter for operations. Profit before one-offs and tax rose to ₹138.3 Cr from ₹112.5 Cr a year ago. But an ₹80 Cr one-off charge (VAW receivables, clearing-agency foreign currency, and ₹18.3 Cr of Awuko closure costs) cut reported profit to just ₹12 Cr.

Return on capital collapsed

The profit earned on each ₹100 of capital employed (ROCE) fell from 20.3% in FY24 to 7.0% in FY26, as one-off charges of ₹104 Cr (FY25) and ₹135 Cr (FY26) hit the books.

In FY26 the Indian parent alone earned ₹416 Cr, but the whole group earned only ₹168 Cr — a ₹248 Cr gap caused by overseas subsidiaries.

Growth levers (the bull case)

2x

Group target to double revenue — the five-year plan (May 2025) implies roughly ₹11,000 Cr by FY30. "Aspiration 2030" programmes were reported on track as of Q2 FY27.

SEMI

Semiconductor ceramics — the Hosur facility has qualified global equipment-maker anchor customers. Management expects revenue to pick up from FY28 and peak at ₹300–350 Cr by FY30–31.

SOFC

Fuel cells for data centres — CUMI supplies a leading solid-oxide fuel-cell maker doubling capacity from 1 GW to 2 GW. Ceramics grew 36.1% YoY in Q2 FY27, fastest of all segments.

EXPORT

Electrominerals exports — standalone exports doubled in FY26 to over a third of segment sales, helped by EU anti-dumping duties on Chinese grains. Target: 40% of sales.

EXIT

Closing loss-makers — Awuko is in final liquidation (assets sold for ₹5.34 Cr in Oct 2026); Foskor Zirconia's exit is expected by Dec 2026. Together they address ₹100–120 Cr+ of yearly losses, lifting group profit from FY28.

FIX

Rhodius turnaround — Q2 FY27 loss narrowed to EUR 0.3 mn from EUR 0.7 mn the previous quarter; break-even targeted by Q1 FY28.

CAPEX

Spending plan — ₹400 Cr consolidated capex maintained; cash tied up in working capital expected to come back in H2 FY27.

The honest read: revenue growth is real and broadening, but profit recovery is a FY28 story. FY27 is a transition year of closures, write-downs and ramp-up spending.

Why 133x is misleading

Trailing profit of ₹126 Cr is shrunk by one-off write-downs. Adjusting for VAW impairments and Rhodius/Awuko losses (normalised earnings), the P/E falls to roughly 45–55x.

What the market is really using

On operating profit before interest, tax and depreciation (EV/EBITDA), CUMI at 32.0x trades slightly below its own 3- and 5-year medians (34.3x) and the industry (34.0x). The market is valuing the operations, not the damaged profit line.

Audit record

  • Clean (unqualified) audit opinions from Price Waterhouse every year FY2021–FY2026.
  • Revenue recognition tested as a Key Audit Matter — no exceptions found.
  • No single customer above 10% of revenue in any year FY19–FY25.
The caveat: VAW (Russia). This sanctioned subsidiary holds ₹1,190.78 Cr of group assets (22.5%) and ₹297 Cr of trapped cash. It has had no independent director since February 2025; SEBI granted an exemption in June 2025, valid until VAW leaves the US sanctions (SDN) list.

The FY26 impairment test's base case (40% weighting) assumes VAW is removed from the sanctions list by January 2027. This governance structure is shaped by geopolitics, not choice — but it needs watching.

Key risks

What could keep profits down or force more write-offs.

One-off charges, three years running

1. Russia / US sanctions — the big one

VAW was put on the US Treasury's OFAC sanctions (SDN) list on 10 January 2025, blocking dollar/euro transactions and hitting export revenue hard. ₹104.13 Cr was written off in FY25 on trapped foreign-currency balances and export receivables; ₹360 Cr of VAW cash cannot be sent back to the group.

The goodwill test leans on de-listing by January 2027, using a 26–28% discount rate. If that slips, ₹148 Cr of goodwill plus the trapped cash are at risk of further write-downs.

2. European drag

Rhodius lost ₹45 Cr in FY26 (vs ₹0.4 Cr in FY25) and Awuko lost ₹75 Cr. Abrasives' profit margin fell from 7.0% to 4.3%. Awuko's closure is largely done, but Rhodius' recovery depends on a weak German industrial cycle.

3. Electrominerals margin squeeze

Profit margin fell from 16.9% (FY23) to 5.6% (FY26) on sanctions, power costs and cheap imports. Power-hungry fused minerals face pressure from Chinese producers.

4. One-offs keep recurring

₹104 Cr (FY25), ₹135 Cr (FY26), ₹80 Cr (Q2 FY27 alone) — reported earnings have been unreliable for three straight years. Group return on capital (ROCE) is 6.6% TTM, far below the historical 18–20%.

5. Cash collection slowing

Customers take longer to pay: receivable days rose from 49 (FY23) to 62 (FY26). Cash from operations covered only 0.67x of cumulative operating profit (EBITDA) over the review window.
