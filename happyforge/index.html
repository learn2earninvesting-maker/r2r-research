The one-screen view

What the company is, what's changing, and the report's verdict.

Happy Forgings is stepping up from automotive parts into very heavy industrial parts through a ₹650 Cr heavy-forging plant plus a newly approved ₹500 Cr expansion. Sales grew 32.7% in Q2 FY27, and profit before interest, tax and depreciation has stayed above 30% of sales (EBITDA margin) for four straight quarters.

What's strong

  • Margins of ~30–31% — roughly double a typical auto-parts peer (15–18%).
  • Low debt: borrowings are only 0.23x of shareholder equity (D/E), with AA credit ratings.
  • Growth has sped up every quarter: +10.4% → +20.4% → +27.0% → +32.7% YoY.
  • Orders already signed for the new heavy products (~₹180 Cr crankshafts, ~₹300 Cr data-centre engine parts).

What to watch

  • The stock costs 50.5x its earnings vs a 36.8x historical median — the expansion is already priced in.
  • Cash left after capex (free cash flow) was slightly negative in FY26 (−₹9 Cr).
  • The heavy-forging revenue mostly arrives in FY28–FY29, and its start date has already slipped once.
  • Exports to US/Europe trucks and tractors remain soft.

Business & segments

What it makes, who buys it, and why rivals find it hard to copy.

Founded in 1979 in Ludhiana as a bicycle-pedal maker, Happy Forgings is now India's fourth-largest engineering-led maker of heavy forged and precision-machined parts — crankshafts, front axle beams, steering knuckles and differential cases for trucks, tractors and industrial equipment. One integrated platform serves five end-markets.

Diversification is deliberate: trucks (CV) fell from over 42% of revenue two years ago to 37% in FY26 — not because trucks shrank, but because other segments grew faster. That makes the business less tied to one cycle.

STRUCTURALIndustrials

Grew from ~2% of revenue in FY21 to 14% in FY26 — large crankshafts for heavy generator sets and pinions for wind-turbine gearboxes. Management sees it reaching roughly 30% of revenue over time.

SCALINGPassenger Vehicles

From 0.9% of revenue in FY24 to 9% in H1 FY27, including a seven-year US electric-SUV contract (machined axle seats, steering knuckles) running to FY32. Gross margins here exceed 70%.

More value per tonne

Machined (finished, ready-to-fit) products made up 90% of H1 FY27 revenue vs 88% in FY26 — the company captures more of the value chain.

Exports

Direct exports were 19% of revenue in H1 FY25; direct + deemed + indirect exports are roughly a quarter of finished-goods sales. H1 FY27 exports contributed 28% of revenue.

The moat: big presses, micron-level machining, integrated plants

  • Press fleet few can match: a 14,000-tonne press (only the second company in India with one that size), three 8,000-tonne presses, a new 10,000-tonne press and ten hammers — forging parts from 3 kg to 250 kg today.
  • Precision: tolerances as fine as 5 microns using Landis and Junker equipment and 5-axis machining centres.
  • Capacity: 158,000 MT forging and 82,000 MT machining across Kanganwal and Dugri.

How full are the plants? (H1 FY27 utilisation)

Forging spare room is deliberate; machining is nearing its peak — which triggered the new ₹500 Cr capex.

The pattern: a boom in FY22–23, a flat FY25 (global truck and farm downturn, export customers cutting stock), then re-acceleration into FY27 from domestic demand, passenger vehicles and industrials. Management guides to meeting or beating its FY27 volume growth guidance.

Quarterly YoY revenue growth

#1 · THE BIG ONE₹650 Cr Dugri heavy-forging expansion

  • What: one of the world's largest forging hammers (125T) plus an 8,000T vertical upsetter — 59,000 MT of new capacity for parts weighing 300 kg to 3 tonnes, for power generation (incl. data-centre backup power), marine, mining, high-HP tractors, wind and oil & gas.
  • When: operations start FY28, revenue "largely from FY '29". Plant plus 20 MW captive solar expected complete by Q4 FY28.
  • Orders: ~₹180 Cr signed on large crankshafts (as of Q3 FY26); ~₹300 Cr secured for a dedicated data-centre heavy-engine machining line, contract to 2033. More OEM orders expected early FY28.
  • Economics: single large products can bring ₹100–150 Cr of revenue; asset turns (revenue ÷ assets) of 1–1.2x achievable by 2030.

#2₹500 Cr additional capex (approved Nov 2026)

Spread over FY28–FY29, funded by debt and internal cash, ~70% for machining. Takes total capacity to ~217,000 MT forging and 116,000 MT machining within 2–2.5 years. Trigger: "strong demand visibility, healthy order books, and existing capacity utilisation nearing their peak".

#3₹950 Cr incremental order book

Peak extra annual revenue, delivered over 2–3 years with 80–85% by FY28, led by exports, PV and Industrials. Price earned per kg on this book is ₹340–350 vs ₹240–245 today — a richer mix that should lift margins.

#4PV ramp & domestic share gains

PV revenue nearly doubled YoY in H1 FY27; a 4,000-ton press line for a North American client starts booking revenue from Q3 FY27; target 12–15% of revenue in 3–4 years. Farm-equipment customer share (wallet share) is ~46%; CV share on medium/heavy trucks expected to rise from 32% to 42%.

#5Cost side: captive solar

~33 MW Muktsar solar plant is ~90% built, starting Q4 FY27 — cutting power cost by ~₹30 Cr a year (~1.5% margin uplift).

Caveat: the heavy-forging step-up is an FY28–FY29 story. FY27 growth rests on the existing plants, whose export leg remains soft — European truck orders showed only "slight improvement, not much" as of Q3 FY26.

FORThe premium has a quality basis

~30–31% margins are about double the typical auto-parts peer (15–18%), with ~17% ROCE and a balance sheet close to net cash (D/E 0.23x).

AGAINSTThe multiple assumes execution

At 50.5x vs a 36.8x median, buyers are paying today for the ₹650 Cr + ₹500 Cr capex cycle to deliver. Negative FCF (FY26: −₹9 Cr on ₹455 Cr capex) and a PEG of 2.55 leave little cushion if Dugri ramps slowly.

The re-rating has largely happened: the +81.6% one-year return has outrun even the strong earnings pickup — TTM profit growth of ~22.8% trails the expansion in the P/E.

Promoter & governance

Who owns it, how the family runs it, and where shareholders have pushed back.

Shareholding (Sept 2026 quarter)

Promoter 78.44% (78.54% a year earlier) · zero pledged shares.

Skin in the game is unambiguous: near-total promoter ownership, no shares pledged as loan collateral, and 92% of the ₹1,393 Cr capex programme funded from the company's own cash.

Structure

A single listed operating company with one dormant wholly owned subsidiary (HFL Technologies, incorporated March 2024, not yet operating). No questionable group entities.

Family interlock

Chairman & MD Paritosh Kumar (founder) is the father of MD Ashish Garg; Whole-Time Director Megha Garg is Ashish Garg's wife.

WATCHGovernance friction

  • Continuing Paritosh Kumar as Chairman & MD beyond age 70 drew 21.15% of public institutional votes against (July 2025 AGM).
  • The employee stock option (ESOP) ratification drew 35.13% against in 2024.
  • Minor: ₹14,160 fines each from BSE/NSE for a six-day gap in the Nomination & Remuneration Committee; an Audit Committee warning for an inadvertent contra trade.

Risks

Strong credit profile, but exposed to cycles and to delivering the capex on time.

CREDITBest-in-class for a standalone company

CRISIL AA/Stable reaffirmed on ₹485 Cr of bank facilities (Sep 2025); ICRA AA (Stable) / A1+ reaffirmed (Jul 2026). Both upgraded from AA− in early 2024 — among the strongest ratings for a company this size without a big-group parent.

Debt is rising, but still modest (₹ Cr)

Borrowings ₹143 Cr (FY24) → ₹529 Cr (TTM). Intermediate years NOT DISCLOSED in the report. D/E 0.23x.

HIGHDomestic cyclicality

Trucks + farm equipment are ~65–70% of revenue, so volumes follow the heavy-truck cycle and monsoon/rural incomes. FY25 proved it: revenue grew just 3.7% when global truck and farm markets fell.

HIGHCapex execution

The 125T hammer is brand-new equipment for the company. Timing language slipped from "production commencing by end of FY27" to operations commencing FY28, with revenue "largely from FY '29".

MEDIUMExport & tariff exposure

US and European truck and tractor demand stayed weak through FY26, with tariff uncertainty adding to customer destocking. Management is awaiting "further clarity on the fine prints" of India–US trade developments.

MEDIUMCapital intensity & cash flow

FY26 capex ₹455 Cr vs operating cash flow ₹445 Cr (FCF −₹9 Cr). FY27 capex guidance ~₹400 Cr ex-solar (~₹480 Cr incl. solar), plus the new ₹500 Cr programme.

MEDIUMCustomer concentration

Top 10 customers are 68% of revenue; one customer exceeds 10%. Steel-price increases are passed on, but other cost inflation is still being negotiated with customers.

LOWGovernance & contingent liabilities

Institutional dissent at two consecutive AGMs (see Promoter tab). ₹99.15 Cr of disputed tax matters with no provision made (FY24 disclosure).
