Core Theme: the grid supercycle

Why India's power-transmission build-out is the story behind this stock.

A pure-play bet on India building and upgrading its power grid — with exports and data centers as extra engines.

Who they are

The listed Indian arm of GE Vernova's global grid business, manufacturing in India since 1958. Roughly half of India's power flow is managed through its technology.

Strategy: "India for the World" — make high-voltage equipment locally, build complete substations for Indian utilities, and export to 60+ countries.

Why the theme works now

  • CAPEXTransmission spending doubling. India's transformer capacity is expected to nearly double from 14.5 lakh MVA (Mar 2026) to 24.1 lakh MVA by FY32 — about ₹9.2 trillion of investment.
  • EXPORTSGlobal shortage. The world is short of high-voltage equipment, opening export doors that didn't exist last cycle. Export demand leans to higher-value products (HVDC, grid modernisation, GIS), which helps prices and margins.
  • HVDCThe long-distance power-line era. Won its first big HVDC (high-voltage direct current) project in FY26 — the 2.5 GW Khavda–South Olpad link — plus a 2×500 MW Chandrapur refurbishment.
  • DATA CENTERSData centers need grid hookups. India has under 2 GW of data-center capacity versus 13–18 GW expected by 2030 — a grid-connection opportunity of ~₹85,000–1,05,000 Cr for the sector.

Business Segments: three engines

What the company actually sells.

1Turnkey substations

  • Designs, supplies and switches on complete high-voltage substations at a fixed price. Revenue is booked as work progresses (percentage-of-completion); an average project takes ~24 months.
  • 98% of the backlog is with private developers, central utilities (Power Grid, NTPC) and PSUs. State electricity boards (often slow payers) are under 2%.
  • Builds both open-air (AIS) and compact gas-insulated (GIS) substations, 132 kV to 765 kV. Commissioned 26 substations in FY25 alone.

  • Exports = 33% of FY26 revenue (up from 17% in FY20) and 8% of the order book. New markets: Europe, South-East Asia, Middle East, Africa.

Latest quarter & margins

  • Q2 FY27: profit +44% to ₹431 Cr on total income of ₹2,077 Cr; operating margin held at 27.1%.

Growth Levers & Catalysts

What could drive the next leg of growth.

Management guides 30%+ revenue growth a year for the next 3–4 years. Only ~₹1,000 Cr of capex is needed, so extra revenue flows largely to profit (operating leverage).

HVDCBarmer ±800 kV — lowest bidder (L1)

Power Grid confirmed L1 status on 7 Sep 2026 for a 6,000 MW terminal station. The company's share in a typical HVDC project is 40–60%, so one win of this size can add ₹3,000–5,000 Cr in a peak execution year.

HVDCKhavda–South Olpad (Adani)

2,500 MW, ±500 kV VSC terminal; ~4-year execution, with most revenue landing FY29 onwards.

HVDCPipeline & localisation

  • 2–3 projects expected to tender in 2027; ~10 Rajasthan links of ~6 GW each through 2035–36; the National Electricity Plan targets 33 GW of new bipole capacity by 2032.
  • ₹140 Cr to restart the Pallavaram LCC valve line, add a VSC-STATCOM valve line and a Noida controls facility — making the full HVDC product made in India.

EXPORTUS data-center transformers

~$151 mn related-party approval for US transformer exports (valid till 15 May 2027); under negotiation, conclusion expected H2 FY27. Vadodara is GE Vernova's only higher-rating transformer plant in Asia.

STATCOMGrid-stabiliser wave

The CEA recommends STATCOMs (devices that keep voltage stable) at all future 400/765 kV substations handling renewables. A sizable chunk of India's awards is expected in Q4 FY27.

ORDERSIntake tracking

H1 FY27 new orders of ₹3,580 Cr annualise to ~₹7,160 Cr — within the ₹7,000–8,000 Cr base target.

Expensive in absolute terms, but cheaper than its own 3- and 5-year history. A PEG of 0.73 (price-to-earnings divided by growth) says the 30%+ growth isn't fully priced in.

The bull read

A 71.6× P/E against ~44% TTM profit growth is defensible if execution holds — and it's roughly in line with the industry (69.2×) despite far better growth and returns.

The bear read

At 38.3× book value and a 1.5% free-cash-flow yield, there's no margin of safety if growth slips to 15–20%. The multiple shrinking (derating) is the single biggest valuation risk.

What looks clean

  • Royalty to parent is steady: FY26 ~₹190 Cr (~3% of revenue) vs ~₹140 Cr (~3.3%) in FY25; formula unchanged for years.
  • Free parent guarantee: GE Vernova Inc. guarantees up to ₹5,100 Cr for cash-pool lending to LM Wind and "shall not charge any guarantee commission".
  • Clean audits: seven straight unqualified opinions from Deloitte; reappointment proposed through 2031.
Watch items: sales to group companies (related-party) rose to 29% of total sales in FY26 (from 18.3%). Two customers are ~21% and ~11% of FY26 revenue. The parent guarantee behind cash-pool lending ends 31 Dec 2026.

Key Risks

What could break the thesis.

HIGHCash lent to the group

The company is "debt-free" but lends to a GE group cash pool (₹5,783 Cr inter-corporate deposit at Mar 2025; ₹971 Cr outstanding at Mar 2026). It's backed by a parent guarantee that expires 31 December 2026. Shareholder approval for higher lending limits is pending.

HIGHMultiple derating

At 71.6× earnings and 38.3× book, any growth disappointment (HVDC tender delays, STATCOM slippage) would hit the share price harder than it hits earnings.

MEDRaw-material swings

Exposure to CRGO steel and copper; management has acknowledged "winners' curse" dynamics on Power Grid bids.

MEDCustomer concentration

Two unnamed customers make up ~21% and ~11% of FY26 revenue.

MEDLumpy HVDC revenue

Multi-year projects pay on milestones, so quarterly numbers jump around. The US data-center order has already slipped from Q1 to H2 FY27.

LOWLitigation tail

Contingent liabilities include ₹267 Cr of GST/sales-tax demands and a draft income-tax order of ₹37.25 Cr plus interest. A Chennai leasehold defect has appeared in every audit report since FY22.

LOWPromoter selling overhang

Promoters have already cut their stake by 24 points. Further sales — for shareholding rules or parent capital needs — can't be ruled out.
