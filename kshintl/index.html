Snapshot

The whole story in one screen: what's working, what isn't, and what to watch.

Bottom line: KSH is a genuine beneficiary of a long, structural boom in transformer and HVDC (high-voltage direct current — long-distance power lines) spending. But profits have grown while cash has not, and at 45.6x earnings the market is paying in advance for that cash problem to get fixed.

Theme: picks-and-shovels on India's transformer boom

What KSH makes, why customers stick with it, and the demand wave behind it.

KSH makes magnet winding wires — the precision copper conductors inside every large transformer, motor and generator. It is India's largest exporter and third-largest maker of these wires, and the only Indian supplier approved by Power Grid for the special conductor (CTC) used in HVDC 400 kV transformers.

MOATThe CTC advantage

KSH brought CTC (continuously transposed conductor) to India as an import substitute about 20 years ago and still leads. It takes 5–6 precision manufacturing steps versus 2 for ordinary round wire, and makes up over half the specialized wire business.

LOW RISKCopper cost is passed on

Production starts only after a confirmed order (make-to-order). The copper price (LME) and currency moves are passed straight to the customer, so KSH earns a fixed processing margin per tonne with no copper-price bet.

Management: the copper price is fixed at the start of each order, so there is no real fluctuation for that order.

CUSTOMERSSticky blue-chip franchise

120+ manufacturers including Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, GE Vernova, ABB and CG Power. Repeat business is above 95%, and the oldest relationship goes back close to 40 years.

DEMANDThe tailwind

Indian transformer makers are expected to nearly triple capacity to 300 GVA by 2027-28, in line with India's ₹9.1 trillion transmission spending plan. Winding-wire order books across the sector stand at ~₹70,000 Cr through FY27.

Credit rating upgrade

In February 2026, CARE upgraded the long-term rating to CARE A; Stable and short-term to CARE A1 (from CARE A- / CARE A2), reflecting lower debt after the IPO.

Capacity doubling

CATALYSTHVDC orders

Cumulative orders for 37 HVDC transformer windings, including 11 from BHEL for the Khavda/Nagpur project — the first domestic HVDC winding-wire sourcing in four years, and the highest value-add product in the range.

IN PROGRESSHitachi Energy framework

A five-year supply framework with Hitachi Energy Global covering Indian and some global plants is signed. Quantity and price terms are still being finalized.

CATALYSTExports re-accelerating

Exports grew 76% YoY in Q1 FY27 (92% in Q4 FY26). Exports are ~27% of revenue versus a 40% historical high; management targets returning there within two years.

OPTIONALITYEV wires & recycling

Exclusive India licence with HPW Metallwerk for patented PEEK-coated EV motor wires — the highest profit per tonne in the range, lines live around end-Q2 FY27. Plus a 5,000 MTPA copper recycling facility at Chakan, commissioned August 2026.

Risks: the cash conversion problem

Why profits on paper haven't turned into cash, and the other risks to watch.

The Achilles heel: cash from operations was negative in 4 of 5 years. Over FY22–FY26, cumulative operating cash flow was ₹-70 Cr against cumulative profit of ₹286 Cr — a conversion ratio of -0.28x. Fast growth ties up money in unpaid customer bills (receivables) and stock (inventory).

Fix in motion: management is moving copper buying from 100% advance payment to credit terms, targeting 30–35 net working capital days. Average days improved from 71 to 60 in Q1 FY27.

HIGHShort-term debt reliance

Total borrowings fell to ₹316 Cr in FY26, but current (short-term) borrowings rose to ₹290 Cr — mostly on-demand working capital loans secured against inventory, receivables and liened deposits.

MEDIUMCustomer & sector concentration

Top 10 customers are ~51% of revenue and the power sector dominates. Diversifying via EVs, 24 export countries and cross-selling.

MEDIUMCopper swings

Passing copper costs to customers removes price risk but not cash risk — sharp copper spikes inflate the money needed to run the business mid-cycle.

MEDIUMEPCG export obligation

₹201.76 Cr of export obligations starting April 2026 under the duty-saving capital goods scheme (EPCG). ₹33.63 Cr of duty saved is at risk, plus interest and penalties, if unmet.

FLAGRevenue recognition

The FY26 auditor flagged revenue recognition as a key audit matter for the first time after the IPO.
