The answer up front

What the report concludes, in one screen.

The bull side
HFCL just reported its best quarter ever. Sales were ₹2,542 Cr (up 143.6% from a year ago), profit was ₹394 Cr (up 448%), and it kept ₹24.8 of every ₹100 of sales as operating profit (operating margin, OPM). A global boom in fibre for AI data centres is driving it, and it has ₹28,911 Cr of orders waiting to be delivered (order book).
The trade-off
The company has a long history of missing its own targets (guidance), its business used more cash than it brought in during FY26 (negative operating cash flow), and the promoter/governance record needs watching.
For
  • A real theme shift: AI fibre demand, more export sales, defence as an extra upside (optionality).
  • The numbers back it this time: ₹28,911 Cr of orders, 24.8% OPM.
  • Management now guides for at least 80% sales growth over FY26.
  • The stock is clearly in an uptrend (Stage 2).
Against
  • Nine years of profits not turning into cash.
  • Promoter group buying via warrants at ₹74 while the stock trades at ₹269.
  • Large money flows with a weaker subsidiary (related-party transactions).
  • Valuation — 46.8x P/E, 8.4x P/B, negative free cash flow — assumes flawless delivery on the biggest contracts in its history.
Bottom line
The uptrend (Stage 2) is unambiguous. Whether the business deserves the price the market is paying for it (the multiple) is the open question. This is an analysis, not a buy or sell recommendation.

Four engines at different stages

What HFCL makes, and which parts are growing.

HFCL designs and makes the physical parts of networks — optical fibre, cables, radios, routers and defence electronics — in its own Indian factories. It has deliberately moved away from low-margin "build it for you" contracting (turnkey EPC) towards selling its own products.

Products vs projects — share of revenue

Products 27% (FY21) → 83% (Q1 FY27). Projects 73% (FY21) → ~38% (FY26).
Growth engine

Optical fibre & cable (OFC)

  • India's largest OFC supplier. Makes its own fibre at Hyderabad; fibre capacity doubled to 2.8 crore fibre-km, cable capacity ~4.3 crore fibre-km.
  • One hyperscaler (a giant cloud/data-centre company) contract worth ~USD 1.1 billion (~₹10,159 Cr) over five years locks in roughly half or more of cable capacity. Deliveries began June 2026 at ~USD 200 million a year.
  • Moat in Europe: the EU found HFCL was not selling below fair price (no dumping) — the only Indian OFC maker exempt from EU anti-dumping duties.
  • Making its own raw material (backward integration): a 300 MT preform plant (preform is the glass rod fibre is drawn from), ₹580 Cr capex, targeted by July 2029. In-house preform cuts fibre cost 18–20%.
Steady

Telecom & networking equipment

  • Indian-designed IP/MPLS routers (56–800 Gbps), Wi-Fi 6/7 access points, and 5G home-internet boxes (FWA CPE).
  • Routers have won ₹800 Cr+ of BharatNet Phase III orders.
  • R&D centres work with Qualcomm, Broadcom and Intel. Approved for up to ₹652.79 Cr of PLI incentives (a government subsidy linked to output).
Optionality

Defence & aerospace

  • Own-designed electronic fuzes (NATO certified), thermal weapon sights, surveillance radars, multi-mode hand grenades. Hosur facility running since Dec 2024.
  • Order book ~₹2,300 Cr. Management says it is on track for ~₹500 Cr of defence revenue in FY27.
  • A 1,000-acre Andhra Pradesh defence complex (329 acres allotted) and an aerospace acquisition expected to close this quarter.
Shrinking legacy

EPC / system integration

  • Has laid over 2,00,000 km of fibre, including the ₹8,098 Cr armed forces network.
  • Fell from 73% of revenue (FY21) to ~38% (FY26). Now mainly a channel to install HFCL's own products.

A violent inflection

Flat-to-falling for three years, then a sharp turn.

The turn
Sales fell from ₹4,727 Cr (FY22) to ₹4,065 Cr (FY25) and margin shrank to 11.1%. FY26 was the turn; FY27 is the breakout.

Theme & catalysts

Why demand is rising, and what could drive the next leg.

The theme
AI data centres have rewired the fibre cycle. Selling more abroad (export mix) is the margin story.

Fibre price index (CRU)

Record low 79 (Mar 2025) → 263 (Mar 2026), after 32 months of decline.

What's driving demand

  • AI data centres: Q2 FY27 growth came from AI data-centre demand for optical fibre cable. G.657A2 fibre prices are up 30%+.
  • China-plus-one: US tariffs on Chinese fibre make HFCL more competitive. Export orders were ₹12,248 Cr at end-FY26.
  • At home: BharatNet Phase III (₹65,000 Cr programme) is underway; Make-in-India defence buying supports the defence business.
  • Mix shift: exports rose from 4.5% of revenue (FY21) to 41.4% (FY26), guided to 60–65%. Prices realised expected up ~25% in FY27 on very high fibre-count cables (7,000-fibre developed; 14,000-fibre being commercialised).

Catalysts ahead

Contract

The $1.1B hyperscaler deal

~₹10,159 Cr over five years — about 1.7x FY26 revenue — with deliveries from June 2026.

Visibility

Order book ₹28,911 Cr (Sep 2026)

~5.8x FY26 revenue. Product orders are ~65% of the book vs 22% in FY25; over half of product orders are exports.

Capacity

Expansion

  • Fibre capacity to 3.4 crore fkm and cable to 4.3 crore fkm by Dec 2026.
  • IBR cable (the format hyperscale data centres use) expanding from 0.17 to 1.9 crore fkm.
  • ~₹1,800 Cr announced across fibre, cable and preform.
New line

Data-centre connectivity

₹215 Cr facility for 2,70,000 assemblies a year by Sep 2027. ~₹850 Cr of orders in hand; FY27 revenue guided at ~₹800 Cr.

Optionality

Defence ramp

₹500 Cr FY27 aspiration; ammunition complex groundbreaking in May 2026; aerospace acquisition closing this quarter with a ₹2,000 Cr+ order book.

Margin lever

Preform plant

300 MT plant by July 2029 cuts fibre cost 18–20% while preform prices are rising 20–25%.

Credibility caveat
Of 47 tracked guidance items, FY24–FY25 saw repeated misses: FY25 growth guided 25–30%, delivered −9%; FY25 OFC guided ₹2,000 Cr, delivered ~₹1,100–1,200 Cr; FY26 defence guided ₹200+ Cr, delivered ₹77 Cr; Poland plant put on hold. FY27's targets are far bigger — execution is the bet.

What the multiples say

  • Price vs profit (P/E) 46.8x: below its 3-year median but well above the 5-year median of 38.1x. ET quotes 64x vs a 5-year average of 39x on its own basis.
  • Price vs book value (P/B) 8.4x: more than double the industry's 3.8x. The market is paying for the shift to products, not for the assets.
  • Free cash flow yield −1.5%: current earnings aren't yet cash, so the price rests entirely on FY27–29 delivery.

Takeaway
Cheaper than the industry on P/E and EV/EBITDA, but very expensive on book value, with no free cash yet. You are paying for future delivery.

What can break the story

The issues most likely to derail the thesis.

Profit vs cash, FY26 (₹ Cr)

Same year: ₹550 Cr raised via QIP and a dividend recommended.
01

Cash conversion is the core problem

Over 9 years, cash from operations (CFO) totalled ₹972 Cr — only 0.50x of total profit. FY26 operating cash flow was −₹378 Cr and free cash flow −₹608 Cr against profit of ₹329 Cr.

02

Money stuck in working capital

Supplier-finance liabilities of ₹434 Cr, with credit periods of 120–240 days vs contractual 30–120. Consolidated debt up from ₹748 Cr to ₹1,713 Cr over three years.

03

Few big customers

The top 3–4 customers make up more than 60% of revenue (Nahata, Q2 FY25 call). Reliance Jio has been a client for 7–8 years.

04

Guidance credibility

A multi-year record of misses across defence, OFC, exports and PLI.

05

Defence is still a promise

FY26 defence revenue ₹77 Cr at a −6.58% PBIT margin (a loss at segment level). Fuzes took five-plus years from "almost developed" to trials. The aerospace acquisition is still pending.

06

Trade & demand risks

The EU exemption was hard-won. Nuvama flags a global slowdown in data-centre spending and preform delays as key risks.

07

Surveillance tag

Exchanges placed HFCL under LT ASM Stage 4 (extra monitoring for abnormal volatility and speculation). The Nivetti stake-sale deadline was extended to 31 Dec 2026.
